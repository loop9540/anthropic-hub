# Filing Requirements for Canadian Public Companies — TSX Venture Exchange (TSXV) and Canadian Securities Exchange (CSE)

*A study of the regulatory and exchange filing obligations of a reporting issuer in Canada, focused on junior issuers listed on the TSXV and the CSE.*

*Last updated: August 2026. This is an informational study, not legal advice. Rules change frequently — confirm against the current instruments, exchange policies, and counsel before relying on any deadline.*

---

## 1. The regulatory architecture

Canada has **no single national securities regulator**. A public company answers to several bodies at once:

| Body | Role | What you file there |
|---|---|---|
| **Provincial/territorial securities commissions** (BCSC, ASC, OSC, AMF, etc.), coordinated through the **Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA)** | Securities law: continuous disclosure, prospectuses, insider reporting, enforcement | Everything filed on SEDAR+ and SEDI |
| **The exchange** (TSXV or CSE) | Listing requirements, review/acceptance of transactions, timely disclosure policy, tier maintenance | Exchange forms, notices, fees |
| **CIRO** (Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization) | Market surveillance for both TSXV and CSE; trading halts for pending material news | Advance notice of material news releases during/near trading hours (via the exchange's market surveillance process) |
| **Corporate statute** (CBCA, OBCA, BCBCA, etc.) | Annual meetings, shareholder financial statements, corporate registry filings | Annual returns, registered office/director changes |

Key mechanics:

- A company becomes a **reporting issuer** in each province where it has filed a prospectus (or via exchange listing/other triggers), and owes continuous disclosure in every one of those jurisdictions. The **passport system** (MI 11-102) lets most filings be cleared through a **principal regulator** — normally where the head office is located.
- **SEDAR+** (launched July 25, 2023, replacing SEDAR) is the mandatory electronic filing system for virtually all securities-law filings: financial statements, MD&A, news releases, material change reports, prospectuses, technical reports, exempt distribution reports. System and regulator fees are paid through it.
- **SEDI** is the separate electronic system for insider trading reports.
- Ontario reporting issuers also pay an annual **OSC participation fee** based on capitalization.

### Venture vs. non-venture status — the single most important classification

Under securities law (NI 51-102), a **"venture issuer"** is a reporting issuer *not* listed on the TSX, Cboe Canada (formerly NEO), a U.S. national exchange, or certain other senior markets. **Both TSXV and CSE issuers are venture issuers under securities law**, which gives them longer filing deadlines and lighter disclosure obligations.

One nuance: since its April 3, 2023 policy overhaul, the CSE has a senior tier of **"NV (non-venture) issuers"**. NV Issuers remain venture issuers under the securities-law definition, but **CSE policy contractually imposes non-venture-equivalent obligations on them** (90/45-day financial statement deadlines, an annual AIF within 90 days, majority voting for directors, and stricter governance). Everything below flags where the NV tier differs.

## 2. Continuous disclosure under securities law (applies on both exchanges)

These obligations come from CSA national instruments and apply regardless of which exchange the company trades on.

### 2.1 Periodic filings — NI 51-102

| Filing | Venture issuer (typical TSXV / CSE issuer) | Non-venture (and CSE NV tier by policy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual audited financial statements** + annual **MD&A** | Within **120 days** of financial year-end | Within **90 days** |
| **Interim financial statements** + interim **MD&A** (Q1, Q2, Q3) | Within **60 days** of quarter-end | Within **45 days** |
| **Annual Information Form (AIF**, Form 51-102F2**)** | **Not required** (file voluntarily to gain short-form prospectus eligibility under NI 44-101) | Required within 90 days |
| **Executive compensation disclosure** (Form 51-102F6V for venture) | Within **180 days** of year-end (or in the information circular) | Within 140 days (Form 51-102F6) |
| **CEO/CFO certifications** (NI 52-109) | Venture forms 52-109FV1 (annual) / FV2 (interim), filed with each set of statements — no representations on ICFR/DC&P design | Full forms F1/F2 including internal-control representations |

Notes:

- A venture issuer **without significant revenue** may replace full interim MD&A with the streamlined **"quarterly highlights"** disclosure permitted by Form 51-102F1.
- Statements must be approved by the board (interims may be approved by the audit committee) before filing; the auditor's report accompanies annual statements.
- Missing a deadline typically leads to a **cease trade order (CTO)** or, if applied for in advance under NP 12-203, a **management cease trade order (MCTO)** restricting only management while the company catches up, with required bi-weekly default status news releases.

### 2.2 Event-driven filings

| Trigger | Obligation | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Material change** in the business | News release **forthwith**, then a **Material Change Report** (Form 51-102F3) on SEDAR+ | MCR within **10 days** of the change (confidential filing possible, renewed every 10 days) |
| **Significant acquisition** | **Business Acquisition Report** (Form 51-102F4) | Within **75 days** of closing. Venture threshold: acquisition must exceed **100%** of the issuer's size (asset or investment test); non-venture: 30% with a two-trigger test |
| **Material contract** entered outside the ordinary course | File the contract on SEDAR+ | With the AIF, or within 120 days of year-end (venture) |
| **Change of auditor** | Reporting package (notices + letters) under NI 51-102 Part 4.11 | News release + filings at the time of change |
| **Change of year-end, corporate structure, chief executive, etc.** | Notices under NI 51-102 | As specified per event |
| **Mining disclosure** | NI 43-101 technical report supporting first-time disclosure of mineral resources/reserves or other triggers | Generally within **45 days** of the triggering disclosure |
| **Oil & gas issuers** | Annual NI 51-101 filings (51-101F1/F2/F3) | With/by the annual filing deadline |

### 2.3 Shareholder meetings and proxy materials

- Hold an **annual meeting** (corporate statutes generally require one within 15 months of the last, and within 6 months of year-end under the CBCA).
- File and deliver a **Notice of Meeting, Information Circular (Form 51-102F5) and form of proxy**; comply with **NI 54-101** for delivery to beneficial owners (notice-and-access is available). File materials on SEDAR+ no later than the date they are sent.
- The circular carries the **corporate governance disclosure** (Form 58-101F2 for venture issuers), **audit committee disclosure** (Form 52-110F2 for venture issuers; a venture issuer's audit committee needs 3 members, a majority of whom must not be executive officers — full independence majority is a non-venture requirement), and usually the executive compensation disclosure.
- File a **report of voting results** after the meeting.
- Send annual/interim financial statements to registered and beneficial holders who request them (annual request form regime).

### 2.4 Insiders and significant shareholders

| Obligation | Who | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Insider reports on SEDI** (NI 55-104) | Reporting insiders (directors, CEO/CFO/COO, 10%+ holders, etc.) | Initial report within **10 calendar days** of becoming an insider; each subsequent trade within **5 calendar days** |
| **Early warning** (NI 62-104 / NI 62-103) | Any acquirer crossing **10%** of a class | News release promptly (before opening of trading next business day) + early warning report within **2 business days**; repeat at each ±2% move or material change of intention |
| **Related-party transactions** (MI 61-101, adopted in ON, QC, AB, MB, NB) | Issuer | Formal valuation and/or minority shareholder approval unless exempt; both TSXV (Policy 5.9) and CSE incorporate MI 61-101 for all their issuers |

### 2.5 Financings under securities law

- Private placements rely on prospectus exemptions in **NI 45-106** (accredited investor, minimum amount, family/friends/business associates, listed-issuer financing exemption, etc.). After closing, file a **Report of Exempt Distribution (Form 45-106F1)** within **10 days** in each jurisdiction where purchasers reside, with fees.
- The **Listed Issuer Financing Exemption (LIFE)** allows freely tradeable shares for modest raises subject to a short offering document (Form 45-106F19) filed on SEDAR+.
- Most exempt-market shares carry a **four-month-and-a-day hold period** (NI 45-102).
- Prospectus offerings (long form, or short form if AIF-qualified) are filed and receipted through SEDAR+ under NI 41-101 / NI 44-101.

## 3. TSX Venture Exchange — exchange-level requirements

The TSXV's rulebook is the **Corporate Finance Manual** (numbered policies + forms). The exchange pre-clears most corporate actions: the general posture is **"file first, get Exchange acceptance, then close."** Filings are increasingly made through the TSXV's electronic systems (e.g., **V-File** for private placements).

### 3.1 Structure and maintenance

- Two tiers: **Tier 1** (senior, higher listing and maintenance thresholds, lighter escrow) and **Tier 2** (most juniors). **Policy 2.5** sets continued-listing ("tier maintenance") requirements; failure can move an issuer to the **NEX board**.
- **Annual sustaining fee**, scaled to market capitalization, plus per-transaction filing fees.
- **Personal Information Forms (PIFs)** and background checks for every new director, officer, promoter or other insider — Exchange acceptance is required for board/management changes.

### 3.2 Timely disclosure (Policy 3.3)

- Material information must be disclosed **immediately** by news release through an approved wire service.
- News with potential market impact released during or near trading hours must be **pre-notified to Market Surveillance (CIRO)** so a halt can be considered.
- The Exchange's materiality standard ("material information") is broader than the securities-law "material change" standard — some events require a news release even when no MCR is required.

### 3.3 Transaction filings (the day-to-day workload)

| Transaction | Key policy / forms | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| **Private placement** | Policy 4.1; Form 4A (price reservation), **Form 4B** (notice) | Reserve price; file Form 4B and request conditional/final acceptance within **30 days** of price reservation or the price reservation lapses; file closing materials for final acceptance. Pricing rules (minimum $0.05, discounts to market price, warrant terms) apply. Filed via **V-File** |
| **Finder's fees, loans, bonuses** | Policy 5.1 | Exchange acceptance; caps on cash/warrant finder's fees |
| **Shares for debt** | Policy 4.3 | Prior Exchange acceptance of settlement price and creditors |
| **Security-based compensation** | Policy 4.4 | Plan must comply with plan categories (e.g., rolling 10%); shareholder approval as required (rolling plans re-approved annually); grants reported/filed with the Exchange |
| **Acquisitions/dispositions of assets** | Policy 5.3 | Notice/filing with supporting valuation of consideration; "Reviewable Transactions" need prior acceptance |
| **Reverse takeover / change of business** | Policy 5.2 | Full re-listing-style review: sponsorship (unless exempt), disclosure document (filing statement or circular), shareholder approval, trading halt, escrow |
| **Normal course issuer bid** | Policy 5.6 | Notice filed and accepted before purchases |
| **Name change, consolidation, stock split, dividend** | Various | Prior notice to and acceptance by the Exchange before announcement/effectiveness |
| **Escrow / seed share resale** | Policy 5.4 (and NP 46-201) | Value or surplus escrow releases over 18–36 months; escrow transfer needs consent |

### 3.4 Practical rhythm on the TSXV

There is **no monthly reporting** to the TSXV. The recurring burden is: (i) securities-law periodic filings (Section 2), (ii) transaction-driven exchange filings with their acceptance cycles, (iii) the annual sustaining fee, and (iv) keeping insider/PIF matters current.

## 4. Canadian Securities Exchange — exchange-level requirements

The CSE brands itself a **disclosure-based** exchange: less transactional pre-approval than the TSXV, but **more standing disclosure posted on the CSE's own website** through its issuer portal. The rulebook is a compact set of numbered **Policies** with associated **Forms**. Major amendments took effect **April 3, 2023**, including the **NV (non-venture) issuer tier** and advance-notice requirements for financings and acquisitions.

### 4.1 Standing/recurring filings unique to the CSE

| Filing | Deadline | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 7 — Monthly Progress Report** | Posted **before the open of trading on the 5th trading day of each month** | What the issuer did in the prior month: business progress, securities issued, management changes, related-party transactions, etc. |
| **Form 6 — Certificate of Compliance** | Accompanies the monthly posting | Certification that the issuer is in compliance with CSE requirements and securities law |
| **Updated Form 2A Listing Statement** | On a fundamental change | The listing statement is the CSE's core disclosure document and must be refreshed for RTO-style transactions |

(The old **Form 5 quarterly listing statement update was eliminated** years ago — the monthly Form 7 is the recurring exchange document.)

### 4.2 Financings and share issuances

- **Form 9 — Notice of Issuance or Proposed Issuance of Listed Securities**: since the 2023 amendments, an issuer must **publicly announce a private placement or share-based acquisition and post the Form 9 at least 5 days before closing**; if the CSE does not object within the window, the issuer may close, then files closing confirmation/treasury order details. Price reservation requests follow Policy 6 (Distributions & Corporate Finance).
- Securities-law filings (Form 45-106F1 within 10 days, hold periods, LIFE) apply exactly as on the TSXV.

### 4.3 Other CSE obligations

- **Timely disclosure**: immediate news releases for material information, posted through the CSE portal as well as SEDAR+; pre-notification to market surveillance (CIRO) for halts, as on the TSXV.
- **PIFs (Form 3)** for new directors/officers.
- **Fundamental changes** (RTO-equivalents): shareholder approval, updated listing statement, exchange review — but **no sponsorship regime** and generally lighter deal review than the TSXV.
- **Notices** for name changes, consolidations, symbol changes, dividends before announcement.
- **Fees**: initial listing fee plus **monthly sustaining/maintenance fees** (contrast with the TSXV's annual sustaining fee).

### 4.4 CSE NV (non-venture) issuers

An issuer that meets senior listing standards (or grows into them) is designated an **NV Issuer** and must, by exchange policy:

- File annual statements/MD&A in **90 days** and interims in **45 days** (i.e., senior-issuer deadlines);
- File an **AIF within 90 days** of year-end;
- Adopt **majority voting** for uncontested director elections;
- Meet stricter governance and shareholder-approval thresholds.

This tier was designed to open CSE-listed names to institutional investors whose mandates exclude "venture" securities.

## 5. Model compliance calendar (venture issuer, December 31 year-end)

| Date | Obligation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly, 5th trading day** (CSE only) | Form 7 + Form 6 posted before the open | CSE policy |
| **Ongoing** | News release + MCR (10 days) for material changes; insider SEDI reports (5 days); Form 45-106F1 (10 days post-closing); exchange notice/acceptance before corporate actions | NI 51-102, NI 55-104, NI 45-106, exchange policies |
| **April 30** (120 days) | Annual audited FS + annual MD&A + FV1 certifications | NI 51-102 / NI 52-109 |
| **May 30** (60 days after Mar 31) | Q1 FS + MD&A (or quarterly highlights) + FV2 certifications | NI 51-102 |
| **~May–June** | Circular, proxy, notice of meeting for AGM (file on SEDAR+ when sent); AGM held; voting results filed | NI 51-102 / NI 54-101 / corporate law |
| **June 29** (180 days) | Executive compensation disclosure (51-102F6V) if not in the circular | NI 51-102 |
| **August 29** | Q2 FS + MD&A + certifications | NI 51-102 |
| **November 29** | Q3 FS + MD&A + certifications | NI 51-102 |
| **Annually** | TSXV sustaining fee / CSE monthly fees; OSC participation fee if an Ontario reporting issuer; corporate annual return | Exchange / OSC / corporate law |

For a **CSE NV issuer**, the periodic deadlines compress to March 31 (annual, 90 days, plus AIF) and 45 days for each quarter.

## 6. TSXV vs. CSE at a glance

| Dimension | TSXV | CSE |
|---|---|---|
| Securities-law status | Venture issuer | Venture issuer (NV tier assumes senior obligations by policy) |
| Periodic deadlines | 120 / 60 days | 120 / 60 days (NV: 90 / 45 + AIF) |
| Recurring exchange report | None | Form 7 monthly progress report + Form 6 certificate |
| Financing process | Prior acceptance model: Form 4A price reservation, Form 4B within 30 days, conditional → final acceptance (V-File) | Notice model: Form 9 posted ≥5 days before closing; CSE non-objection |
| Deal review (RTO/COB) | Heavy: sponsorship, filing statement, halt, escrow | Lighter: fundamental-change policy, updated listing statement, no sponsors |
| Tiers | Tier 1 / Tier 2 (+ NEX for inactive) | Regular / NV Issuers |
| Sustaining fees | Annual, market-cap scaled | Monthly |
| Governance overlays | Corporate Finance Manual policies (options plans, finders, escrow, minority protection 5.9) | Compact policies; MI 61-101 incorporated; majority voting for NV |
| Typical issuer profile | Mining, energy, tech juniors; CPC (capital pool company) program | Cannabis, mining, tech, life sciences; historically faster/cheaper listings |

**The big picture:** roughly 80% of the compliance load (financial reporting, MD&A, certifications, MCRs, circulars, insider and early-warning reporting, exempt distribution reports) is identical on both exchanges because it flows from CSA instruments. The exchanges differ mainly in *transaction mechanics* (TSXV pre-clearance vs. CSE notice-based) and the CSE's *monthly reporting* obligation.

## 7. Consequences of non-compliance

- **Late periodic filings** → placement on the reporting-issuer default list; **CTO or MCTO** (NP 12-203); exchange may suspend or move the issuer (TSXV → NEX).
- **Unaccepted corporate actions** (e.g., closing a placement without TSXV final acceptance or before the CSE Form 9 window runs) → trading halts, unwinding, fines, and refusals of future filings.
- **Insider reporting failures** → late fees per insider per issuer (e.g., OSC/BCSC late fees accrue daily) and enforcement.
- Persistent failures → delisting and loss of reporting-issuer exemptions.
